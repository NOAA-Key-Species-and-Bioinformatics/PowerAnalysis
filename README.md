A simulation analysis was completed to evaluate the statistical power needed for coral embryo viability experiments, specifically how many tubes of 10 embryos would be needed to have a 80% chance to detect a change in viability. Each tube could contain a fixed or random number of coral embryos bounded by user-defined minimum and maximum values. For each simulated tube, embryo viability was modeled as a binomial process in which the number of viable embryos was sampled from a binomial distribution using a tube-specific probability of viability. Tube-level heterogeneity was incorporated by applying a normally distributed random effect on the logit scale to the baseline viability probability. Specifically, the baseline control probability was transformed using the logit function, a random tube effect was added, and the adjusted probability was converted back to probability space using the inverse logit transformation. This approach allowed the simulations to incorporate biologically realistic variation among tubes while preserving probabilities within the interval from 0 to 1.

Statistical comparisons between control and treatment groups were then performed using Welch’s two-sample t-test applied to tube-level viability proportions. Welch’s t-test was selected because it does not assume equal variances between treatment groups and is appropriate when tube-level variability may differ among experimental conditions. Statistical power was estimated through Monte Carlo simulation by repeatedly generating independent experiments and recording the proportion of simulations in which the treatment effect was detected at a significance threshold of α = 0.05. Power curves were generated across a range of tube replication levels to estimate the minimum number of tubes required per treatment group to achieve a target statistical power of 0.80. Simulations were repeated for multiple treatment effect sizes, allowing evaluation of the relationship between experimental replication and the magnitude of detectable reductions in embryo viability.

Artificial intelligence tools, including ChatGPT and Google Gemini, were used to assist in drafting the Python simulation code, analyzing and reviewing the computational framework, and reviewing preliminary versions of the Methods text associated with the power analysis; all outputs were critically evaluated, edited, and verified by the author prior to inclusion in this report.
